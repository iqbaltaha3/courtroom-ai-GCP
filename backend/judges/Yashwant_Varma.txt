JUSTICE YASHWANT VARMA
Former Judge, Allahabad High Court (resigned 9/10 April 2026)
======================================================

STATUS NOTE: Varma resigned effective 9-10 April 2026, amid impeachment
proceedings in Parliament, and is therefore no longer a sitting judge. He is
included here because he was on the Allahabad HC permanent-judges roster as
of the May 2026 Dept. of Justice list snapshot referenced, and because his
case is the single most significant controversy involving any judge in this
seniority band - of clear investigative interest.

BASIC DETAILS
- Date of Birth: 6 January 1969, in Prayagraj (Allahabad)
- Enrolled as Advocate: 8 August 1992
- Elevated as Additional Judge, Allahabad HC: 13 October 2014
- Confirmed as Permanent Judge: 1 February 2016
- Transferred to Delhi High Court: 11 October 2021
- Transferred back to Allahabad HC (amid controversy - see below), took oath:
  5 April 2025
- Resigned, effective: 9/10 April 2026 (letter dated 9 April, submitted to
  President Droupadi Murmu; announced 10 April)
- Scheduled retirement had been: 5 January 2031
- Source of appointment: Bar
- Family background: reported to be a second-generation judge - his father
  also served on the Allahabad High Court.

EDUCATION
- B.Com (Honours), Hansraj College, Delhi University.
- LL.B., Awadhesh Pratap Singh University ("Rewa University"), Madhya Pradesh
  (graduated 1992).

CAREER TIMELINE
- 8 August 1992: Enrolled as an advocate; built a varied civil-side practice
  before the Allahabad High Court covering constitutional law, labour and
  industrial legislation, corporate law, taxation, and environmental law.
- 2006-2014: Special Counsel for the Allahabad High Court.
- 2012-August 2013: Chief Standing Counsel for the State of Uttar Pradesh;
  designated a Senior Advocate by the Court in August 2013.
- 13 October 2014: Elevated as Additional Judge, Allahabad High Court.
- 1 February 2016: Took oath as Permanent Judge, Allahabad High Court.
- Known during his Allahabad/Delhi tenures for an assertive courtroom
  approach, including a willingness to question investigating agencies such
  as the Enforcement Directorate and the Income Tax Department (per Maktoob
  Media profile).
- 11 October 2021: Transferred to the Delhi High Court, where he became the
  third senior-most judge.

THE CASH-DISCOVERY CONTROVERSY (2025-2026) - CENTRAL EVENT OF HIS CAREER
- 14 March 2025, ~11:35 pm: A fire broke out at Justice Varma's official
  residence on Tughlak Road/Tughlaq Crescent, Delhi (he was reportedly not
  present; family members alerted the fire brigade and police).
- Fire and police personnel responding to the blaze allegedly discovered
  "four to five semi-burnt sacks" of Indian currency notes in a storeroom -
  reported amounts ranged as high as approx. Rs 15 crore in various press
  accounts, with stacks reportedly over 1.5 feet high in places.
- Delhi Fire Services Chief Atul Garg initially stated publicly that no cash
  was found, then reportedly reversed this account the following day,
  adding to public confusion over the facts.
- Justice Varma has consistently and strongly denied the allegations,
  stating no cash was ever placed in the storeroom by him or his family, and
  has characterised the affair as "a conspiracy to frame and malign him." He
  later told a parliamentary panel he was not present during the fire and
  denied any cash was recovered.
- 21 March 2025: The Chief Justice of India (then Sanjiv Khanna) initiated a
  formal in-house inquiry; a three-member committee was constituted -
  Justice Sheel Nagu (Chief Justice, Punjab & Haryana HC), Justice G.S.
  Sandhawalia (Chief Justice, Himachal Pradesh HC), and Justice Anu
  Sivaraman (Karnataka HC).
- 22 March 2025: In an unprecedented step, the Supreme Court publicly
  uploaded the preliminary inquiry report of Delhi HC Chief Justice D.K.
  Upadhyaya, including photos and video, plus Justice Varma's written
  response.
- 20 & 24 March 2025: Supreme Court Collegium meetings recommended
  transferring Varma from Delhi HC back to his parent court, Allahabad -
  a move that drew strong protest from the Allahabad High Court Bar
  Association and Bar Association members, who opposed hosting a judge
  under this cloud.
- 28 March 2025: President approved the transfer; Centre notified it.
- 5 April 2025: Took oath as Judge of the Allahabad High Court; his judicial
  work was withdrawn pending the inquiry.
- 4-5 May 2025: The three-judge in-house committee submitted its report to
  the CJI, examining roughly 50 witnesses (fire and police personnel, Delhi
  Fire Services chief Atul Garg, Delhi Police Commissioner Sanjay Arora, and
  others). Reports (citing the Times of India) described the report as
  containing "damning evidence" that cash was found at the residence, and
  the panel found Varma and his family had control over the room in
  question.
- 8 May 2025: Then-CJI Sanjiv Khanna forwarded the inquiry report to the
  President and Prime Minister, opening the door to possible impeachment.
- 21 July 2025: Over 140-200 (reports vary) Members of Parliament across the
  Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha signed a motion seeking Varma's impeachment/
  removal.
- August 2025: Varma challenged the constitution of the parliamentary
  inquiry panel before the Supreme Court, arguing the Rajya Sabha Deputy
  Chairman had separately rejected a related removal motion; the Supreme
  Court dismissed his plea and allowed the process to continue.
- 12 August 2025: Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla formally accepted the motion
  (146 MPs) and constituted a three-member committee under the Judges
  (Inquiry) Act, 1968, comprising (reports vary slightly on names/spelling
  across outlets) Justice Aravind/Amit Kumar, Justice Maninder Mohan
  Srivastava, and senior advocate B.V./B.B. Acharya, to formally examine the
  charges as a precursor to a possible impeachment vote.
- 9 April 2026: Varma submitted his resignation to President Droupadi Murmu,
  addressed also to CJI Surya Kant, stating he did not wish to "burden" the
  office with his reasons but tendered his resignation "with deep anguish."
- 10 April 2026: Resignation announced/took effect, formally rendering the
  parliamentary removal proceedings "infructuous" (moot), since such
  proceedings lapse once a judge leaves office. Legal commentators (e.g.
  senior advocate Indira Jaising) noted that, post-resignation, Varma loses
  the immunity attached to judicial office and in principle becomes liable
  to ordinary criminal prosecution over the cash allegations.

POLITICAL/PUBLIC REACTION
- The affair triggered rare cross-party consensus in Parliament on pursuing
  removal, and was raised in the Rajya Sabha by Congress leader Jairam
  Ramesh, who pressed for stronger judicial-accountability measures.
- Commentators and legal bodies (e.g. Moneylife's "Unanswered Questions"
  piece) raised open questions that were never fully resolved in public
  reporting reviewed here, including who removed fire debris from the scene
  on the morning after the fire and on whose instructions.
