JUSTICE SHEKHAR B. SARAF
Judge, Allahabad High Court (Administrative Judge, Agra district)
======================================================

BASIC DETAILS
- Date of Birth: 21 October 1971
- Family: Son of the late Justice Sohan Lal Saraf and the late Sulochana
  Saraf (i.e., second-generation judge - his father was also a judge).
- Elevated as Additional Judge, Calcutta High Court: 21 September 2017
  (first National Law University graduate to be elevated to that court)
- Confirmed as Permanent Judge, Calcutta HC: 16 September 2019
- Transferred to Allahabad High Court: notified 13 November 2023, took oath
  17 November 2023
- Date of Retirement: 20 October 2033
- Source of appointment: Bar
- Sitting at: Lucknow

EDUCATION
- Schooling: St Xavier's Collegiate School, Calcutta, and La Martiniere
  School for Boys, Calcutta.
- 1996: B.A. LL.B. (Hons.), National Law School of India University (NLSIU),
  Bangalore - among the first graduating classes of India's premier
  National Law University.
- 2000: Chevening Scholarship, "Young Indian Lawyers Program," sponsored by
  the UK Foreign and Commonwealth Office; studied for six weeks at a UK
  institution (College of ... - full institution name not fully confirmed
  in this pass).

CAREER TIMELINE
- Notably the first NLSIU student to undertake a placement with Dipankar
  Gupta, then Solicitor General of India, in New Delhi.
- July 1996-December 1997: Worked with Arthur Andersen & Co. (global
  consulting firm) on corporate law and indirect taxation matters.
- January 1998: Joined the chambers of Senior Advocate R.N. Bajoria at the
  Calcutta High Court, practising in taxation matters.
- 12 March 1999: Enrolled as an advocate in the Bar Council of West Bengal
  (seniority as advocate backdated to 19 January 1998).
- 2010: Appointed the Law Ministry's Senior Panel Counsel at the Calcutta
  High Court.
- 2016: Co-founded a stipend-support initiative for incoming NLSIU
  freshers.
- 21 September 2017: Elevated as Additional Judge of the Calcutta High
  Court - the first National Law University graduate to become a judge of
  that court.
- 16 September 2019: Confirmed as Permanent Judge of the Calcutta High
  Court (unusually rare for an additional judge to be given such an
  extension before confirmation, per contemporaneous legal press).
- 3 August 2023: Supreme Court Collegium (headed by CJI D.Y. Chandrachud)
  proposed his transfer to the Allahabad High Court "for better
  administration of justice."
- 7 August 2023: Saraf accepted the proposal but simultaneously requested
  reconsideration, asking in the alternative to be transferred instead to
  Delhi, Hyderabad, Bangalore, or Chandigarh.
- 10 August 2023: The Collegium found no merit in his request and reiterated
  its recommendation to send him to Allahabad.
- 13 November 2023: Centre notified the transfer (one of five High Court
  judges transferred that day).
- 17 November 2023: Took oath as Judge of the Allahabad High Court.
- Currently serves as Administrative Judge for the Agra district and
  regularly sits at the Lucknow Bench.

NOTABLE INCIDENT - PUBLIC DISCLOSURE OF CORRUPTION APPROACH (Calcutta HC,
pre-transfer)
- In an incident widely covered in the legal press (Bar & Bench "Spotlight"
  series, March 2022), Justice Saraf recused himself from hearing a case
  after publicly revealing in open court that a lawyer had approached him
  in relation to that ongoing matter seeking a favourable order. He stopped
  short of naming the individual but stated clearly that such attempts at
  corruption "would not be tolerated." This was reported as an unusually
  candid public disclosure by a sitting High Court judge.

NOTABLE JUDGMENTS (Calcutta HC period)
- Held that a victim reimbursed by a non-statutory source is not entitled
  to a "double benefit" in an insurance matter.
- Held that a Supreme Court interim stay of a High Court judgment does not
  obliterate the binding effect of that judgment.
- Issued guidelines on how courts should handle writ cases mistakenly filed
  on the wrong administrative side.
- Held that courts are institutions tasked to adjudicate, not legislate,
  when considering when judicial inquiry is warranted.
- Ruled on the power to set aside an ex parte decree under the CPC on
  grounds of fraud.
- Held (2023) that an arbitral award passed by a unilaterally appointed
  arbitrator is void.
- Held that Section 11 jurisdiction (arbitrator appointment) is not a mere
  cosmetic exercise to examine the existence of an arbitration agreement.

NOTABLE JUDGMENTS (Allahabad HC period, since Nov 2023)
- Quashed tax orders passed without hearing an assessee who had cancelled
  their GST registration.
- Referred to a larger bench the question of whether the Allahabad HC can
  hear a Section 29A application (Arbitration Act) to extend time for
  arbitration.
- Pulled up a GAIL employee found secretly recording court proceedings on
  a phone.
- Held that costs imposed in frivolous cases are meant to deter abuse of
  process, not to discourage legitimate claims.
- Ruled on the taxation of franchise agreements.
- Directed the UP government to create dedicated legal teams to avoid delay
  in filing appeals.
- Ordered the state to compensate the family of a minor rape victim without
  further delay, and separately ordered action against officials over delay
  in a minor rape survivor's compensation, directing an additional Rs 2
  lakh in relief.
- Directed the UP government to pay Rs 10 lakh to the family of a minor who
  died by suicide in jail (Feb 2026).
- Persuaded a teenage girl and her relatives to avoid a risky abortion
  (2024) - a matter involving delicate medical/reproductive-rights
  considerations.
- Rejected an advocate's plea challenging Leader of Opposition Rahul
  Gandhi's Lok Sabha election from Raebareli.
- Quashed a "permanent residents only" condition for e-rickshaw registration
  in Lucknow as violative of the right to livelihood.
- Held that a transfer policy is only a guiding factor and not legally
  enforceable, and that courts cannot direct a state to transfer an
  employee.
- Explained the correct procedure for removal of a "Nagarpalika" (municipal
  body) president under the UP Municipality Act.
- Directed Meta to take down "defamatory" content targeting religious figure
  Swami Ram Bhadracharya.
- Asked how self-styled godman Rampal was managing to write and distribute
  books allegedly targeting Hindu deities from Hisar prison.
- Held that an unsuccessful tenderer cannot challenge tender conditions at
  the final stage, and that tender challenges rooted in business rivalry or
  hurt pride must be resisted, with narrow scope for judicial review in
  contractual/tender matters generally.
