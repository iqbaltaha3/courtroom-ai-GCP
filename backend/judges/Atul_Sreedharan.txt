JUSTICE ATUL SREEDHARAN
Judge, Allahabad High Court
======================================================

BASIC DETAILS
- Date of Birth: 24 May 1966
- Appointed Additional Judge, Madhya Pradesh High Court: 7 April 2016
- Confirmed Permanent Judge, MP High Court: 17 March 2018
- Transferred to Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh HC: April 2023, oath May 2023
- Transferred back to Madhya Pradesh HC: March 2025
- Transferred to Allahabad High Court, took oath: 11 November 2025
- Date of Retirement: 23 May 2028
- Source of appointment: Bar

EDUCATION
- Specific university/law school not confirmed in this pass; his professional
  biography (court and press profiles) begins with his joining a senior
  advocate's chamber in 1992, implying a law degree obtained around/before
  that year. Needs a targeted search to confirm.

CAREER TIMELINE
- 1992-1997: Joined the chamber of Senior Advocate Gopal Subramanium in
  Delhi; assisted in civil and criminal matters before the Supreme Court,
  Delhi High Court, and Delhi trial courts.
- 1997-December 2000: Independent practice in Delhi.
- 2001: Shifted to Indore, began independent practice before the Indore
  Bench of the Madhya Pradesh High Court; closely associated with Senior
  Advocate Satyendra Kumar Vyas. Also handled criminal and some civil cases
  before trial courts, service matters and writ petitions, and medical
  negligence cases before consumer forums.
- 2005: Appointed Central Government Counsel.
- 2010-2013: Central Government Counsel (Senior Panel), appearing in cases
  for the Union of India. Also served as Panel Advocate/Government Advocate
  for the State of Madhya Pradesh at the Indore Bench.
- 7 April 2016: Appointed Additional Judge, Madhya Pradesh High Court.
- 17 March 2018: Confirmed as Permanent Judge, MP High Court; became part of
  the MP High Court Collegium.
- 23 January 2023: Requested his own transfer out of Madhya Pradesh, citing
  that his elder daughter was about to enter legal practice before the same
  courts (District Court and Indore Bench).
- 28 March 2023: Supreme Court Collegium (headed by then-CJI D.Y.
  Chandrachud) accepted his request and recommended transfer to the High
  Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh; he took oath there in May 2023.
- March 2025: Repatriated back to Madhya Pradesh High Court.
- August 2025: Collegium recommended his transfer to the Chhattisgarh High
  Court.
- 14 October 2025: Collegium modified this recommendation - after the
  Central Government sought reconsideration - and instead recommended
  transfer to the Allahabad High Court. Commentators (Bar & Bench) flagged
  this reversal, "in light of the government's interference," as unusual and
  as effectively blocking his path to a Chief Justiceship (at Allahabad he
  ranks around 7th in seniority, versus a senior Collegium-track position he
  would likely have held at Chhattisgarh).
- 18 October 2025: President confirmed the transfer.
- 11 November 2025: Sworn in as Judge of the Allahabad High Court by Chief
  Justice Arun Bhansali.
- At his Madhya Pradesh farewell, he quoted Urdu poet Rahat Indori ("Jo aaj
  sahib-e-masnad hai kal nahi honge... kiraaye daar hain zaati makaan thodi
  hai" - roughly, today's power-holders are only tenants, not owners of the
  house) - widely read in the press as a pointed remark on his own transfer
  history. Several Bar leaders publicly criticised the transfer as a blow to
  judicial independence.

NOTABLE JUDGMENTS
- 14 May 2025 (Madhya Pradesh HC): Took suo motu cognisance, based on media
  reports and viral video, of a "scurrilous" speech by sitting BJP minister
  Vijay Shah disparaging Colonel Sofia Qureshi (a senior Indian Army
  officer). A Division Bench of Sreedharan and Anuradha Shukla, JJ., directed
  the MP Director General of Police to register an FIR against Shah by that
  same evening under specific sections of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023,
  and ordered that the court's order be treated as part of the FIR for all
  judicial/investigative purposes. Shah's later apology was rejected by the
  Supreme Court, which upheld the seriousness of the matter (per Hindi-
  language press coverage).
- Bhopal Gas Tragedy: As part of a bench, directed the state to urgently
  examine relocating a toxic-waste containment site away from habitation.
- September 2024 (Jammu & Kashmir/Ladakh HC, per Bar & Bench): Imposed
  exemplary costs of Rs 9 lakh on an administration for evading payments it
  owed.
- 27 April 2026 (Allahabad HC - "Teachers Association Madaris Arabia and 2
  Others v. National Human Rights Commission and 8 Others," Writ-C No.
  32051/2025): In an interim order, harshly criticised the National Human
  Rights Commission (and state human rights commissions generally) for
  failing to take suo motu cognisance of assaults and lynchings of Muslims
  in India, and for instead over-extending its jurisdiction into unrelated
  matters (in this case, madrasa investigations). He specifically cited the
  chilling effect on interfaith social contact ("Even having a cup of coffee
  at a public place with a person of a different religion becomes a fearful
  act sometimes"). His bench-partner, Justice Vivek Saran, publicly disagreed
  with these "sweeping observations" in a rare split interim order, noting
  the NHRC was not represented in court when the remarks were made. Widely
  covered (The Quint, The Wire, Bar & Bench, tax/legal blogs) as an unusually
  candid judicial rebuke of the NHRC.
- 20 July [year not fully confirmed, reported by The Print as during his
  Allahabad tenure]: Delivered a split verdict on the use of writ
  jurisdiction to restrain "bulldozer justice" (extra-legal demolitions of
  homes/businesses of criminal-case accused). Opened his judgment by quoting
  poet Bashir Badr ("People face ruination in just making a home and you
  feel no remorse setting ablaze entire settlements"). Applied Supreme
  Court demolition safeguards, restraining demolition of an accused person's
  house for two years from FIR registration where the action appeared
  retributive, while carving out an exception for genuine public-purpose
  demolitions (with one year's prior notice) for long-standing dwellings.

MEDIA COVERAGE / CHARACTERISATION
- Widely profiled (Bar & Bench "Spotlight" series, The Print, The Quint, The
  Wire) as a judge whose career has been marked by repeated inter-High-Court
  transfers, and by a consistent pattern of rulings curbing perceived
  executive overreach and defending the rights of religious minorities - a
  tendency multiple outlets suggest may have been a factor in his transfer
  history, though this is inference/commentary rather than an established
  fact.
- No personal corruption or ethics controversies were found in this pass;
  the controversy that surrounds him is institutional/political (concerning
  his repeated transfers and their timing relative to his rulings), not
  personal misconduct.
