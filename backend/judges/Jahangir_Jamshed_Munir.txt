NOTABLE JUDGMENTS - EXTENSIVE AND HIGH-PROFILE DOCKET
Justice Munir (referred to in legal press as "Justice J.J. Munir") has one
of the most extensively covered dockets of any judge in this batch, with a
strong recurring theme of holding police and state officials accountable,
and a willingness to make pointed remarks about the judiciary's public
standing. Notable rulings include:

On police/state accountability:
- Slammed a Senior Superintendent of Police (SSP) for deputing a Sub-
  Inspector to reply to a Chief Judicial Magistrate (CJM), calling it
  "downright impertinence" and seeking an explanation.
- Issued a show-cause contempt notice to an SP and a Child Welfare Committee
  (CWC) chairperson for "detaining" a woman despite a stay on her arrest.
- Converted a habeas corpus petition into a criminal writ petition in the
  case of a missing UP police officer feared dead, flagging suspicion over
  "dysfunctional CCTVs."
- Suspecting a custodial death of a man missing since 2018, similarly
  converted a habeas plea to a criminal writ and "slammed" the Director
  General of Police (DGP).
- Revived a 1988 episode in which an SP allegedly "threatened" to drag a
  judge to a police station, seeking his whereabouts and details of any
  action taken - an extraordinary example of judicial memory/accountability
  spanning decades.
- Directed personal appearance of an SSP over failure to provide police
  force to a judge executing a court commission.
- Asked the Union of India whether central agencies could trace a
  "fugitive" concerned with a 1984 appeal, after UP Police failed to do so.
- Held that non-availability of a judge at headquarters "seriously invades"
  a citizen's liberty.

On labour and welfare law:
- Held that part-time police-station sweepers are entitled to minimum wages
  under the Minimum Wages Act, 1948, finding a Rs 1,200 monthly honorarium
  "illusory."

On judiciary's public standing / contempt of judicial officers:
- Framed contempt charges against a man over a WhatsApp message accusing a
  judicial officer of corruption and treason.
- Remarked that the "current trend of public is to malign judges with
  baseless allegations," imposing a Rs 10,000 cost on a litigant, and
  separately observed a "mindset developed among public to overawe judges
  by complaining and maligning them on baseless allegations."
- Deprecated the trend of prosecuting agencies targeting lawyers, saying it
  "undermines [the] judicial system" and warrants the "harshest penalty."
- Explained the correct convention for how retired High Court judges' names
  should be written.

On religion, personal liberty and social issues:
- Ordered protection for a woman who converted from Islam to Hinduism and
  married according to Hindu rites, and separately directed the SSP to
  ensure local police do not interfere in the married life of a Muslim woman
  who had adopted the Hindu religion.
- Held that "people eliminating a family member for choosing a life
  partner" (honour killing) "can't have a place in society," denying bail
  in such a case.
- Denied bail to a man who allegedly raped a woman and posted her obscene
  images on Facebook, daring that "law won't save her" - calling it
  "anti-social activity."
- Denied pre-arrest bail to a professor over a Facebook post against Union
  Minister Smriti Irani, holding the post "may promote ill-will/hatred
  between different communities."
- Held that cohabitation with an adult "husband" invites POCSO liability,
  directing a minor mother to stay in a shelter home until she turns 18; and
  separately held that a minor girl who willingly married cannot be allowed
  to stay with her husband until she attains majority - two rulings
  reflecting a strict approach to the intersection of POCSO and child/minor
  marriage cases.
- In a related custody matter, held that custody of a minor "husband"
  cannot be given to the "wife," as that would sanction cohabitation between
  an adult and a child.
- In a matter involving AMU's Anti-CAA protests, stated that students
  "should not indulge in activities that bring bad name to great
  educational institutions."
- Ruled that acquiring land for road construction on the basis of alleged
  oral consent is not sufficient without proper legal proceedings.
- On family/matrimonial matters: denied a mother (accused of murdering her
  husband) custody of her daughter, while granting her liberty to seek
  custody again if acquitted; held a minor "needs the tender care that only
  a mother can provide" in a separate custody dispute.

On institutional/administrative matters:
- Explained the "faulty" caveat procedure at the Board of Revenue and issued
  broad guidelines to fix it.
- Rejected a proposed layout plan for Chandrashekhar Azad Park in Prayagraj
  prepared by a private surveyor, ordering the state to devise a fresh plan.
- Ruled that a Commercial Court cannot hear a Section 34 Arbitration Act
  challenge to compensation awarded under the National Highways Act.
- Held that teachers can be assigned election duties even before the formal
  issuance of a poll notification (Right to Education Act matter).
- Held that only Parliament can include a caste in the Scheduled Castes
  list, quashing UP government orders that had notified 17 OBCs as
  Scheduled Castes - a legally and politically significant ruling on caste
  classification.
- Issued a contempt notice to a state secretary over failure to decide on
  including the Rajbhar community in the Scheduled Tribes list - another
  caste-classification-linked matter with clear political salience in Uttar
  Pradesh.
- Acquitted a murder accused 38 years after his life-imprisonment sentence,
  finding the deceased "was murdered by someone else" - an extraordinary
  wrongful-conviction case.
- Held that a vigilant tweet by a resident about a COVID-19 norms breach
  cannot constitute a violation under Section 144 CrPC.
- Sought explanation from a police Sub-Inspector after it emerged "no
  police station by that name exists" as furnished in a report.

MEDIA COVERAGE / CHARACTERISATION
- Among the most consistently newsworthy judges in this entire batch: his
  docket combines strong police/state accountability rulings, notable
  caste-classification and religious-conversion rulings with clear
  political salience in Uttar Pradesh, and an unusually candid judicial
  voice on the judiciary's own public standing.
- No personal ethics/corruption controversy involving the judge himself was
  found in this pass - the "controversies" here are about his rulings
  holding others (police, officials, litigants) accountable.
